Pro-54

Polyphonic synthesiser

A faithful continuation of the classic Prophet-style voice — two oscillators per voice, the unmistakable filter, poly-mod and wheel-mod routing, and an onboard delay. Free to download. No account, no unlock codes.

The story

In 1978, Dave Smith and Sequential Circuits released the Prophet-5 — the first synthesizer that let you dial in a sound, save it, and instantly recall it later. Before that, getting a sound back meant physically writing down every knob position and resetting them all by hand. That one idea, programmable memory, changed how synthesizers were used from that point on, and the warm, slightly imperfect analog tone underneath it became a sound in its own right.

The Prophet-5 went on to show up everywhere. Gary Numan built his signature sound around it. Nick Rhodes used it across Duran Duran's early records. Joe Zawinul made it a core voice in Weather Report. Phil Collins leaned on it through his early solo albums, and it turns up across records by Peter Gabriel, Madonna, Michael Jackson, Vangelis, Depeche Mode, Hall & Oates, a-ha, Radiohead, and Taylor Swift, among plenty of others. Close to fifty years on, it's still one of the most recognisable synth tones in popular music.

Native Instruments built their own software version of it, the Pro-Five, then refined it into the Pro-52 in 2000 and, in late 2002, the Pro-53 — widely regarded at the time as one of the best software recreations of a classic analog synth available, right down to the quirks of the original circuit. Its sync lead preset even became a familiar voice in the Portal 2 soundtrack. NI discontinued the Pro-53 in 2020, and for a few years it simply wasn't available to buy or download anywhere.

Years later, the Cmajor team brought the synth back to life by porting an unreleased internal C++ version of the Pro-53 DSP to Cmajor, while also recreating its interface in HTML and JavaScript. That work preserved much of the original structure and became the foundation that made Pro-54 possible.

It's a very special plugin to me — the original Pro-52 and then Pro-53, I've been using since they were released. While I've been lucky to keep using them thanks to Bitwig's 32-bit legacy plugin support, a lot of modern DAWs simply don't support the older VST format. I've wanted an up-to-date, 64-bit compatible version, with a new, resizeable GUI and the possibility of adding some other features. Thanks to the kind work of Native Instruments and Cmajor, I'm really excited to be able to give you a free and open-source (under GPL) CLAP and VST3 version of the plugin.

About
Why Truex

The name Truex came from a biography of American electronic music pioneer Raymond Scott, where he mentioned liking the name ‘Truex’. Somehow the name really struck me too.

I’ve always admired Scott’s adventurous, experimental and technically inventive approach to music, and when it came time to name my audio company, Truex Audio felt like a natural fit — and a small tribute to him.
